## Direct SUMO comparison

The registered Spot arm in SUMO specifies vx, vy and yaw rate (±0.7) plus seven arm joint targets, with the gripper fixed closed. ReLIC generates 12 leg actions and the backend sends arm positions directly to servos. UniFP generates all 19 joint actions, taking world EE requests projected at the current base pose and body rates up to ±0.35, ±0.22 m/s and ±0.5 rad/s. UniFP can also take virtual force. These are comparisons of complete architectures. Keep each robot's own reset posture, history, warmed actuators and policy. [SUMO controller overrides](../../sumo/sumo/controller/overrides.py) · [SUMO optimizer](../../sumo/sumo/controller/optimizer_overrides.py) · [Judo command mapping](../../sumo/.judo-src/judo/tasks/spot/spot_base.py)

- **Cone.** Verified equal compiled object mass, inertia, geom types, sizes, poses, masks, friction and contact settings. Cone priority 6 exceeds both floor priorities of 5. Foot/floor friction and the robot's hand geometry and servo dynamics still differ. Start each policy from the same planar base and cone spawn and give it one second of contact-free neutral warmup. Check actual cone pose/speed and record the root displacement after warmup. For the native block, SUMO has a 2 s horizon, 24 candidates, three linear knots, three elites, noise ramp 3.5, one iteration, elite-mean action and nominal 20 Hz updates. UniFP uses the winning eligible trajectory. The 20 ms live tick quantizes the 50 ms SUMO prefix to alternating two/three ticks. SUMO's 125 ms cutoff can fill a candidate suffix. Count completed physics/policy steps when reporting a definitive workload comparison. [ReLIC pilot](../tests/relic_cone.py)
- **Shared work.** The cone pilot retained SUMO's 2 s horizon and three elites, using eight candidates at 10 Hz for 800 scheduled policy calls/update and 8,000/s, equal to UniFP's 16 × 50. Also retain the unmodified 24-candidate SUMO result. Equal calls need different CPU time because SUMO takes two physics substeps per call and UniFP takes four, with different networks and contact calculations. For the final runtime-matched block, calibrate a SUMO population on development scenes to the measured UniFP planning p95 on the same CPUs, then freeze it. Measure native completion deadlines at 50 ms and 100 ms respectively.
- **Strict evaluation prerequisite.** The installed Judo hierarchical execution API returns policy-tick state, sensors and previous policy output. Its Python wrapper calls `mj_forward` after copying qpos/qvel. That call cannot recover the actual two integrated contact wrenches. Add a local execution observer at the native substep boundary before publishing a SUMO strict-release score. Use wrist/finger contact active at either substep, signed vertical ground support on cone in world N, end-tick angular speed/tilt and the same 25-tick hold and robot thresholds. Keep posture and contact exclusions visible across systems. Contact-free final pose alone is insufficient. [Judo live step](../../sumo/.judo-src/judo/simulation/hierarchical_mj_simulation.py)
- **Box prerequisite.** The registered SUMO box is a 0.508 m cube of mass 1.5 kg with principal inertias 0.1445 kg m². Ground priority 5 overrides box priority 4. UniFP uses a 0.40 × 0.40 × 0.50 m, 1 kg cuboid, physical cuboid inertia, separate box–floor pair and hand friction. Build the common cuboid fixture on both prediction and execution sides of a local SUMO task adapter. Check compiled inertia, spawn clearance, pair friction and impulse direction. This retains UniFP's existing bounds and face dimensions. Set the same XY goal from the settled box center and its physical Z center in SUMO's 3D reward. SUMO penalizes mean object-goal distance, mean gripper distance and squared mean speed with weights 60, 4 and 20 respectively. Its inherited success function reads `goal_pos` and `goal_distance_threshold`, while the registered simplified config supplies `goal_position`. Calling it raised `AttributeError` in the installed version. The external evaluator should implement the explicit goals, robot stability and settling rules. Verify any repaired native metric independently before quoting it.
