# EE tracking and contact discovery on fallen cones

General scene 82 places the cone plate 1.60 m away at a bearing of +53.2° to Spot. The tip points almost directly away, only 7.1° from the outward robot-to-plate ray. With the experimental plate-access cost and paired base–arm proposals, UniFP crossed SUMO's upright threshold at 8.90 s and finished a ground-supported, contact-free hold at 10.0 s. The same controller and start timed out with an independent optimizer seed. The standard cone viewer still uses the 4 cm clearance objective. ## Where the EE paths separate

The first CEM knot repeats the last **issued world target**. Each subsequent linear segment is limited to 0.50 m/s in world XYZ. After executing five 20 ms ticks the planner shifts its winner, mean and variance by 100 ms, then anchors the new first knot again. Moving the sampling bounds preserves the previous request in world coordinates. The physical finger starts the next horizon at its last executed pose. The three paths in the viewer share one winning rollout. Cyan is the raw command, dashed violet is the native issued spherical command decoded using each **pre-action** root position and yaw, amber joins the actual starting finger to predicted **end-of-tick** finger positions. White marks the finger at the instant of replanning. [Sampler](../tools/cem.py) · [Winner and decoder](../tools/box_mpc.py) · [Native projection](../native/world.h) · [Viewer](../tools/view_box.py)

In the yaw frame the sphere center is 0.35 m forward and 0.12 m above the root. UniFP receives a radius in [0.15, 0.55] m, elevation in [−40°, +50°] and azimuth in [−90°, +90°]. Moving or rotating the base changes this mapping even for a stationary world request. Position tracking in training is a soft L1 reward on the finger body origin, competing with stability and locomotion. All six force channels were zero here. In a free-space branch from the original cone warmup, an unclipped 8 cm vertical ramp at 0.40 m/s reached half amplitude at 0.10 s. The physical finger reached 4 cm of differential motion relative to a held-command branch at 0.20 s. Over the last 0.2 s it retained 8.5 cm of differential motion, but both branches retained absolute target errors (18 and 21 cm). Thus ordinary response delay and steady tracking offset both contribute. ## SUMO source and reference executions

In the inspected SUMO checkout (`4cea4e4e`), the cone task uses two sensors. `object_z_axis` is the cone site’s local +Z expressed in world coordinates. `sensor_arm_link_fngr` is the position of the finger site in the object site's frame. The finger site is at the finger body origin. The object site is 0.24 m along the cone axis from the plate, coincident with the COM. Taking the vector norm makes the rotating reference frame immaterial. If `a = (R ez)·ez` and `d` is the 3D finger-to-COM distance in metres, SUMO maximizes **−100 mean(1−a) − 0.5 mean(d)** across the rollout. Both are running means. Its task reward has no terminal term, hand force, collision mask, base motion term or release bonus. Success is one observation with `a ≥ 0.9`. The subclass overrides the base reward/success methods. [Cone task](../../sumo/sumo/tasks/spot/spot_cone_upright.py) · [Reward functions and random reset](../../sumo/sumo/tasks/spot/spot_upright.py) · [Sensor definitions](../../sumo/sumo/models/xml/spot_components/sensor.xml) · [Cone sites](../../sumo/sumo/models/xml/objects/traffic_cone/traffic_cone.xml)

UniFP's `upright_proximity` minimizes exactly the negative of this scalar reward divided by 100 on eligible trajectories. Running the actual SUMO reward on its sensor readings for 500 identical MuJoCo poses in ten 50-tick windows gave maximum discrepancy **1.34 × 10⁻¹⁵**. All UniFP comparisons apply the same independent hard eligibility checks for native failure or any nongripper robot–cone impulse. `upright_release` replaces `d` by `0.30 f` on predicted ticks with `a ≥ 0.9`, where `f` is the fraction of substeps exceeding 1 N net hand force. `upright_buffer` adds `0.02 max(0, 1−g/0.04)²` for the closest predicted leg/torso-to-cone gap `g` in metres. Strict success requires 25 ticks within 10°, angular speed ≤0.25 rad/s, at least half-weight ground support, no active hand contact at any substep and stable Spot. These checks are independent of the objective. [UniFP task](../tools/cone_mpc.py)

## Seed 82 and the experimental controller

Native actuator replay from reset reproduced each recorded pose exactly and matched net hand force within 10⁻¹⁰ N at every policy tick. We integrated actual pair wrenches at 5 ms. In the clearance baseline, **all 7.09 N·s** of hand impulse was on the plate or its feet. The impulse-weighted axial contact location was just 2.1 cm from the plate origin. The signed normal on the cone dotted with its axis averaged +0.915. Hand angular impulse about the COM projected on `axis × up` accumulated +0.224 and −0.660 N·m·s. This measures the hand's contribution to increasing alignment and excludes ground torque. The cone mostly stayed in place. In 120 updates, 97 forecast hand contact, but even the best pose in 1,885 eligible trajectories was 102.26° from vertical against a 102.45° starting angle. SUMO-equivalent and release costs ranked the same winner 120/120 times, and release and clearance agreed 119/120. [Substep wrench replay](../tests/cone_contact_wrench.py) · [Matched ranking probe](../tests/compare_cone_batches.py)

The new `plate_access` cost uses the same COM and running proximity weight. When the finger-to-COM segment intersects the square plate from behind, it charges the shorter distance **around either nearer edge** of the plate, using its actual half-width 0.18415 m and plane at local z = 0.033 m. Otherwise it uses Euclidean distance. This is a point-hand, thin-plate approximation. It corrects an approach distance that can reward pressing against the wrong side of a solid plate. It sets no contact point, base pose or release sequence. Clearance and release terms still apply.

With `--joint-proposals`, four of 16 candidates draw a possible hand location along the **entire** cone with 12 cm isotropic Gaussian spread. Each draws a possible base heading within ±90° of current yaw and a working reach of 0.55–0.85 m. Its implied body displacement and turn over the horizon propose all three velocity components jointly with that EE request. The ordinary bounds, first knot, slew limits, interpolation and native scoring apply before any candidate can win. The mean and incumbent remain in the ordinary population. This introduces no extra production rollout. [Proposal geometry](../tools/cone_mpc.py) · [Shared sampling constraints](../tools/cem.py)

On 82/43 the original cost with proposals, the new cost alone, and a longer 1.5 s baseline all timed out at approximately 102.45°. The combined controller acquired productive lower-shaft contact and released the cone. Its signed normal averaged −0.414. Positive/negative hand tipping impulses were +7.93/−0.38 N·m·s. COM displacement was 0.47 m horizontally, peak hand reaction was 82.6 N, and peak absolute base roll/pitch was 8.76°. The final hand force was zero and the grounded release hold lasted 0.52 s. A control consuming identical proposal random numbers but keeping all 16 ordinary knots still timed out. On the successful run, replacing only the four proposed lanes at each exact snapshot found the proposed winner beat the whole ordinary population on 17/100 updates. The first sub-90° prediction occurred at 7.0 s in an ordinary lane. The paired proposals helped reach a state where conventional Gaussian samples could tip the cone. Retain clearance + release as the default. Plate access and paired proposals solved two additional development cases, but lost a held-out success and showed sensitivity to the optimizer seed. The next comparison should use stalled snapshots from 82/67 and 100/67 to measure how often an eligible sample reaches the shaft and generates positive tipping impulse before changing cost weights or the evaluation budget.
